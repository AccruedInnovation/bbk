# Solution–Outcome Fit

`SolutionOutcomeFit` asks whether the requested intervention is actually fit for the operational outcome.

## Why it exists

A project can complete the requested artifact while failing the real need. Examples:

- build a dashboard versus help an operator recognize and respond to a fault;
- add automation versus reduce total handling time without creating unsafe recovery;
- generate reports versus improve the decision that depends on them.

## Core distinctions

Record, proportionately:

- desired operational outcome;
- actors and affected parties;
- current-state or no-change workflow;
- requested intervention;
- success measures;
- protected floors and constraints;
- facts, assumptions, and open questions;
- plausible alternatives, including no change;
- disposition and authority.

## Typical dispositions

- confirmed fit;
- investigate fit;
- constraint or counterpart required;
- reframe the intervention;
- no change preferred;
- blocked pending authority or evidence.

A fit result is not execution authorization. It establishes whether continued planning around the intervention is justified.
