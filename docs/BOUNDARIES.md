# Authority and responsibility boundaries

BBK structures work; it does not create real-world authority.

## What BBK may establish deterministically

Depending on the operation and supplied records, BBK can establish that:

- an artifact conforms to a schema;
- required identifiers and references resolve;
- a recorded authority field is present;
- a candidate digest matches the subject under review;
- gate dependencies and evidence fingerprints match;
- a package or installation matches its manifest;
- a finding remains open until explicitly dispositioned;
- a material subject change invalidates affected evidence.

## What BBK cannot establish by itself

BBK cannot prove that:

- a recorded person or organization actually granted authority;
- an architecture is wise or an outcome is worthwhile;
- a model is competent enough for a consequential assignment;
- two logical roles were physically independent;
- an external tool, compiler, simulator, provider, or service behaved correctly;
- a target system is safe, secure, legal, compliant, deployable, or fit for operation;
- a passing package qualification authorizes a project release.

## Tools versus effects

Tool availability is not effect authority. A role may have filesystem or network capability while remaining prohibited from modifying the subject, publishing, deploying, migrating, changing controllers, accepting residual risk, or closing a protected finding.

## Models propose; deterministic code commits

Models perform judgment, investigation, design, implementation, and interpretation. Deterministic machinery validates exact state transitions and records where exactness matters. Neither side should silently assume the authority of the other.

## Blueprint relationship

BBK is a practical bootstrap harness influenced by the broader Blueprint method. BBK records and projections do not establish official Blueprint capability, lifecycle, readiness, release, organizational, or Tenex authority.
